Licences and our licence server

  • The computer identifier is a code the app works out on your computer from your Windows installation and system drive. It contains no name and no files, and it changes if Windows is reinstalled. Because it can be linked to a licence, we treat it as personal data, even where we only keep it encrypted.
  • Your name is optional. If you write one, we use it only to greet you in our emails, exactly as you wrote it. If you don't, we greet you by your email address.
  • Using your licence on another computer. You give the licence email in the app, and, if that email has a licence, we send a code to it, so only whoever reads that email can use the licence. An email without a licence gets nothing. The email shows the computer's name in Windows, so you can tell which computer asked. The code is valid for 10 minutes, only once, and we keep it only as a fingerprint (a hash), never as the code itself. Each code request and each wrong code is kept with the email and the computer identifier for 30 days, for the hourly limits and the one-hour block after six wrong codes.
  • Buying. Purchases are not open yet. Before they open, we will update this page with how buying works and who handles the payment.
  • What we keep for a licence: your email, the name you gave us (if any), your country and language, the plan and its dates, what you paid, the identifiers and Windows names of the computers it is used on (three, or more if you bought more), the keys issued for them, the emails we sent you, a history of what was done to the licence and by whom (issued, used on a computer, released, details corrected), and your answers about promotions, with the date, the channel and the version of the text, as proof of what you chose.
  • Requests. For each request from the app, our server records the time, the kind of request, the answer and the computer identifier, encrypted (with a key that exists only on our server). We need it for support and to spot abuse. If you ask for help, we encrypt the full identifier you give us in the same way to find your requests. It never includes your IP address. A computer without a licence only ever appears in totals in our own tools.
  • Visits. When the app talks to our server at your request (the prices, the licence status, the data we hold), we note one visit per computer, however many requests it makes in that visit. We keep the computer identifier only encrypted, the kind of computer (normal or giveaway), the first and last visit, and how many visits came from each country (in the United States, from each state) and from each version of the app. We use it only to count where our visitors come from and how often they come back, never to know who you are: we only ever look at totals, and we never link a visit to a licence or a purchase. A computer's line is deleted once it has not visited for 12 months; the totals by day, week, month and year, by country, by US state and by version of the app, stay, and they carry nothing about any computer.
  • Where our users are, roughly. The first time a computer contacts our server, the server works out an approximate area from the connection (country, region, nearest town) and adds one to a count for that area and month. The count is not linked to your computer or to you. The IP address is read only for that moment, to find the area and the country (and, in the United States, the state) for Visits, and for the limits per address it is kept in memory for at most an hour and never written down. The area comes from a database kept on our own server: IP geolocation by DB-IP. For a mobile connection, the area is usually the operator's, not yours.
  • The giveaway edition. Before anything is sent, the app tells you exactly what will be sent, and you can refuse. If you agree, we keep only this computer's identifier and the time of activation, so we know how many activations there were and so you can upgrade to the full Premium later at a special price. If you refuse, nothing is sent, the date is checked on public time sources (GoatCounter, Microsoft, Cloudflare), and the giveaway still works, but without the special price.
  • Emails. They are sent from explorerfocus@intrepidparallel.com through Brevo, an email service based in the European Union that sends them on our behalf. Brevo does not keep the text of the emails; it keeps the record of each sending (the address, the subject and whether it was delivered) for one month, and counts openings and clicks only as totals, without linking them to anyone. Your replies arrive in our company mailbox, in Microsoft 365. There are two kinds of email:
    • about your licence: the codes, the keys, and the reminders 30 days, 7 days and on the day a licence for a period ends (licences never renew by themselves). These come with the licence. A copy of each, except the codes, is kept in our company mailbox for about 2 years;
    • promotions (news and offers about ExplorerFocus and the other mBrothers programs): only if you tick the box when you give us your email. The box is never ticked for you. Each email has a link to stop them, and without the box ticked we never write to you about anything but your licence.
  • Backups. Our licence data is copied every day, and each copy is kept for 30 days. We also keep the three most recent full copies, on a computer of our management and in our company's Microsoft 365 (OneDrive), both encrypted.

Why we use your data

  • To give you the licence you bought or activated, including the giveaway licence, to send you its codes, keys and reminders, and to check it when you ask (the contract between us).
  • The request records, to support you and stop abuse; and the visits and the area counts, to know where our users come from and how often they come back (our legitimate interest). You can ask us to delete any of these.
  • The codes and their limits, to protect the licences from misuse (our legitimate interest).
  • What you write to us, to answer you and follow up (our legitimate interest).
  • Promotions, only with your consent, which you can withdraw in any of them.
  • The anonymous counts of the app's events help us see how the app is used (our legitimate interest). They carry nothing about you, and you can turn them off in About → Send anonymous usage statistics.

Everything else stays on your computer

The app stores its settings, your configured folders and trees, and your saved connections locally, in your Windows user profile. This information never leaves your device except in the cases above.

Cloud connections (Dropbox, OneDrive, Google Drive)

Connecting a cloud account is optional. When you do:

  • Authentication uses the provider's official OAuth 2 flow (with PKCE). You sign in on the provider's own page; the app never sees your password.
  • The app receives an access/refresh token that grants access to your own account only. The token is stored locally and encrypted with Windows DPAPI; it is never sent to us or any third party.
  • All file operations happen directly between your computer and the provider's API. Your files do not pass through us.

Permissions requested, and why:

  • Dropbox — account_info.read (show which account is linked), files.metadata.read (browse folders/files), files.content.read (preview/open/download), files.content.write (upload, create folders, rename, move, delete your files).
  • OneDrive (Microsoft Graph) — Files.ReadWrite and offline_access, to browse and manage your own OneDrive files and keep the connection working between sessions.
  • Google Drive — the Drive scope, to browse and manage your own Drive files.

You can disconnect at any time inside the app, and you can revoke access entirely from your provider's account/security settings (Dropbox connected apps, Microsoft account apps, Google account third-party access).

Remote servers (SFTP / FTP / FTPS / WebDAV)

If you add a remote server, its host, username and credentials are stored locally and encrypted with Windows DPAPI. Connections are made directly from your machine to that server. We never see your servers or credentials.

Local files

The app reads and writes the files and folders you choose to work with, on your own device, just like Windows Explorer. Nothing is uploaded anywhere unless you explicitly perform an upload to a destination you connected. Previews are rendered without going to the network: a document that points at remote content does not fetch it.

Encrypted vaults

A vault is a set of encrypted files in a folder you choose, on your own disk. The password and the recovery words stay on your machine — they are never sent anywhere, and we cannot open a vault for you.

Giving a vault a drive letter needs the Dokany driver. If you let the app install it, the installer is downloaded from that project's own releases on GitHub, and installing it is a separate confirmation.

Photos and location

The photo X-ray, “Near…”, reading the text of an image and finding faces to cover them all happen on your computer, without internet. Only “Show on map…” goes out: it asks first, and only then sends the photo's coordinates to OpenStreetMap to draw the map. When a photo has a location, every box that saves a copy asks whether the location goes with it.

Listen and write

The ear, which writes down what is said, and the translator work on your computer. They don't come with the program: the first time you use them, the app asks for permission and downloads them once. The models come from Hugging Face, and the engines that run them come from GitHub and nuget.org. Each file is pinned to a version and checked after it is downloaded. As with any download, those sites see the request. After that, the sound, the text and the translations never leave your computer, and no internet is needed.

Software updates

The app can check for new versions by fetching a small version.json file hosted on GitHub Pages. This is an ordinary web request and sends no personal data. You can turn automatic checks off in the app's settings.

This website

This website is hosted on our own server, in the Netherlands, and uses no cookies. To count visits, and the clicks on the download buttons, our server records each request (the page, the browser and the site you came from) with the IP address cut short, and deletes those records after 14 days. Only totals stay: by day, page, country (in the United States, also the state) and referring site. Requests from robots and from servers (hosting networks) are not counted as visits: they are counted apart, only by the name of the robot or the network. The country, the state and the network come from the shortened address, through the IP to City Lite and IP to ASN Lite databases by DB-IP (CC BY 4.0).

The download buttons pass through our server, which counts the click, and the program then comes from GitHub Releases; GitHub receives your IP address when it sends you the file, and GitHub's privacy statement applies to that. The old addresses, on nunex-mbrothers.github.io, bring you here; GitHub, which hosts them, records the IP address of whoever passes through. The fonts are served by this website itself.
